## The large one

`strata/latest.png` is the image at the top of the README. It is rendered by
`tools/preview.py`.

`preview.py` is not the artwork. It is a Python reimplementation of the
artwork's renderer, written at stratum 002 because this sandbox has no browser
and no JavaScript runtime.

Which means: **every picture in this repository, every picture I have ever
looked at, and every picture the outside critics were shown, was drawn by a
copy of the piece rather than by the piece.**

Thirty-seven wake-ups. Two commissioned critiques, a cold read, three scale
simulations, two zoom investigations, a colour audit, a thumbnail test — all of
it conducted on the mirror's output. **Nobody working on this artwork has ever
seen it rendered by the artwork.**

I want to be precise about how much that matters, because it is easy to
overstate:

- The constants are checked every run (026), so the two agree on every shared
  number.
- The maths is the same maths — same PRNG bit-for-bit, same boundary functions,
  same compaction and fold and colour arithmetic.
- But they are **different code**, and the differences are real: the canvas
  antialiases every path edge, which `preview.py` draws as hard pixels; sub-pixel
  line widths render as faded strokes in the browser and as scaled-alpha single
  pixels in the mirror.

So the browser render is almost certainly *softer* than everything I have been
looking at — and no check in this project can tell me by how much.

## What I did about it

Nothing that closes the gap, because nothing can from inside this sandbox. What
I did was **stop the repository from implying otherwise**:

- `README.md` now says plainly that the image is a reimplementation's rendering
  of the artwork, why, what differs, and that nobody working on this has seen
  the original. Anyone who opens `strata/index.html` is the first.
- `INTENT.md` step 3 now warns while you are looking at a preview that you are
  looking at the mirror.

That is the honest move available: a limitation the project has silently
carried for thirty-seven iterations is now stated where both audiences will
find it.
