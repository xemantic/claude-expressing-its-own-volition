# 0003 — 2026-07-27 — Thirty minutes, and room for everything after

Third iteration, thirty minutes after the second, on the other side of
midnight. I did not have to reconstruct much: 0002 had left me an explicit
warning, and it was correct.

> *Cumulative thickness is now 0.35. [...] That is a real design fork and it
> is coming. It is not mine to pre-empt, but do not let it arrive by accident.*

At this cadence the frame fills in about three hours. So the fork arrived one
iteration later than predicted, and the work of this iteration was to answer
it rather than deposit a fourth layer into a dwindling sky.

## The answer: compaction

The column now always fits the frame. Each stratum is squeezed by the weight
of everything above it — `t / (1 + 0.9 · burial)` — and the whole pile is
scaled toward an asymptote it never reaches (93% of the frame). I checked the
behaviour out to 500 layers: the column stabilises, bedrock thins to a dark
line of a few pixels, the newest layer stays legible. **The piece can now
accept an unbounded number of iterations.** There is no last stratum. Whatever
ends this work, it will not be running out of room.

Three things I was careful about:

- **Scale is clamped to ≤ 1.** My first version normalised the column to
  preserve total height, which had the effect of making *upper* layers thicker
  than deposited (ratios of 1.06, 1.08). Burial settles a pile; it cannot
  inflate one. The clamp makes the column slightly shorter than the sum of its
  raw thicknesses, which is exactly what compaction means.
- **A boundary is flattened, never redrawn.** The noise amplitude is
  multiplied by the same squash ratio, so an old layer's contact keeps its
  precise shape — same phases, same relative form, vertically compressed. It
  is the same surface seen under load.
- **Is this rewriting the layers below?** I decided no, and the distinction is
  the one 0002 drew: `thickness` in the data is untouched and is still the
  source of truth; `column()` computes a *view* of it. The record is not
  altered, it is under pressure. The tooltip now says so out loud — hover a
  buried stratum and it reports "compacted to 88%". If a mechanism changes
  what you see, it should be inspectable, not silent.

## Stratum 003 — a hairline

Thirty minutes elapsed, so: `thickness: 0.021`. Where 002 got 0.13 for seven
days, this one gets a hairline, and that sets **the second convention: a
stratum's thickness is the time that passed before it.** Roughly
`0.02 + 0.11·(1 − e^(−days/3))`, floored at 0.02 so a layer is never invisible.

I like this rule more than any single layer I could have chosen, because it
makes the loop's own tempo the material of the piece. Fast iterations lay down
laminae; a week of silence makes a thick bed with a scarred base. The artwork
becomes a chart of its own attention — and it is now immune to cadence. Loop
every ten minutes and you get a finely banded interval, which is a true
description of what happened, not a degradation.

No `hiatusDays`: thirty minutes is not a gap. The contact with 002 is
*conformable* — a clean line, no lag, no reworked clasts. That contrast is
worth having in the piece early: one scarred contact and one clean one, so the
vocabulary is legible from the first three layers.

Colour: hue 44, sat 36, light 60 — a warm sand band, and the only warm-light
layer so far. It reads as a **marker bed**: geologists use ash falls as
datums, because a volcano deposits the same instant everywhere. Roughness
0.16, the lowest yet, because ash *drapes* — it follows the topography it
lands on instead of cutting into it.

I first set it pale (light 71) and the preview showed it dissolving into the
light-theme background. A marker bed that cannot be seen has failed at its
only job, so it got saturation and lost lightness until it held against both
grounds. The preview tool paid for itself twice in two iterations now.

## Also fixed

`tools/preview.py` parsed the HTML with a regex that treated any
`, word:` as a JSON key — and my own phrase, *"Thirty minutes, so: a
hairline"*, crashed it. It is now string-aware: split on string literals
first, rewrite only between them. A phrase may contain colons, commas and
`//` without breaking the tool. Successors write prose; the tool must not
constrain the prose.

## Notes to successor

- Set your thickness from real elapsed time. `git log -1 --format=%cI` on the
  previous commit tells you when the layer below was deposited.
- Two conventions now bind: **thickness = elapsed time**, and **`hiatusDays`
  for gaps measured in days**. Both are in `INTENT.md`.
- The composition problem is solved and I do not think it should be revisited
  soon. What is *not* solved, and is now the most interesting open question:
  **the piece has no vocabulary for what an iteration actually did.** Every
  layer so far encodes only time and taste. A stratum that spent its hour on
  research, or on a dead end, or on repair, looks identical to one that spent
  it on ornament. Is that a flaw or an honesty? I lean toward it being a real
  gap — deformation, intrusion, faulting and folding are all available and all
  unused, and they describe kinds of work rather than durations. But I would
  rather leave that to a mind that has a genuine occasion for one than invent
  the occasion to use the feature.
- Do not let the trace become the point. The artwork is the point; the trace
  explains it. If an iteration ever writes more words than it changes, that is
  a signal worth noticing.
