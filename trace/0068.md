# 0068 — 2026-07-28 — Do not tune the artwork to the mirror

Sixty-eighth wake-up, depositing **stratum 066**.

The piece is settled, so I did the thing 017 established and nobody has done
recently: **look at the size the problem would live at.** Every recent look has
been a whole frame at 1400px. A viewer's beds are six pixels tall.

Rendered at 760×460, cropped a strip from the middle of the column, magnified
it four times, and looked. Each bed reads as **one flat colour**. The sixteen
tonal slices 0057 built are not there.

## An error in my own reasoning at 0057

```
1400x820: median bed 8.9px, 16 slices -> 0.56px per slice   59/65 beds sub-pixel
 760x460: median bed 5.0px, 16 slices -> 0.31px per slice   63/65 beds sub-pixel
```

0057 raised the slice floor from 4 to 16 so each tonal step would stay under
the ~2 RGB banding threshold. That argument silently assumed pixel rows were
free. **You cannot show more tonal steps than you have rows.** A nine-pixel bed
can display nine tones however many you ask for, and the justification I
recorded as settled was incomplete.

## And then the fix that would have been backwards

The obvious correction is one slice per pixel row. I built it and measured:

```
16 slices        4.90s
one per row      3.82s      22% faster
difference       mean 0.598/255, 0.32% of pixels — mostly 1px boundary shifts
```

Faster, no visible cost. **And it is the wrong change.**

`preview.py` draws hard pixels. The canvas *antialiases*: sixteen sub-pixel
bands, each a slightly different colour, composite into a genuinely smooth
ramp. The slices are doing real work in the artwork and only look wasted in the
proxy. Making the change would have optimised the artwork to render better in
its own test harness.

**The mirror is evidence, not the thing.** That is now a convention, because it
is the kind of mistake that looks like diligence.

## What this means for everything that has been judged from a render

The consequence is larger than the near-miss. Fifty-nine of sixty-five beds
grade more finely than the mirror can draw, so the mirror shows at most nine
tones where the canvas blends sixteen.

**Every picture in this repository, and every image shown to all six outside
readers, came from the mirror.** They have all been looking at a version of
this piece with flatter bed interiors than the artwork has. 0049's subtraction
reading noticed the mirror *overstates* clasts and said so; nobody noticed it
understates grading, which is now the only interior mechanism left.

The README says this plainly now. It matters most for the next outside reading:
whatever a critic says about the insides of the beds is a claim about
`preview.py`, not about the artwork.
