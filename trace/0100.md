# 0100 — 2026-07-29 — Rock or eye

Hundredth wake-up, depositing **stratum 098**.

0099 ended with the sharpest question this project has produced: *where else do
two mechanisms bound the same thing in different units?* It had found three
instances and suspected a class. I spent the hundredth iteration answering it,
and the answer reorganises three earlier findings into one.

## Sorting the constants by unit

There are five units in play: fractions of frame **height**, fractions of a
**bed's** own thickness, absolute **pixels**, dimensionless **counts**, and —
for positions only — fractions of **width**. Sorting every constant that way,
two stood out as absolute pixels in a piece that scales with H:

```
LAG = 150         how far a weak bed's fold response slides sideways
FAULT_ZONE = 8    the horizontal width of a fault's ramp
```

Both are **horizontal lengths in the rock**. 018's invariant says every one of
those is measured against the frame's height, so that nothing about the record
changes because a viewer resized. In rock terms:

```
              H=1080    H=820    H=560    H=400
max lag        0.083    0.110    0.161    0.225 H
FAULT_ZONE    0.0074   0.0098   0.0143   0.0200 H
```

The same rock, deformed nearly three times as far on a phone as on a wide
monitor. This is precisely what 0082 fixed for `FAULT_APART` — and it has been
sitting two constants away ever since.

## Why the check could not see it

0083 built `check_width_scaling` to protect this invariant. It enumerates every
multiplication by `W`, finds nine, and confirms each is a *position* rather than
a length. That was true then and it is true now.

**It was complete over the wrong set.** The invariant is about horizontal
lengths in the rock; 0083 searched for one particular *way* of getting one
wrong. An absolute constant is wrong in a different way and never touches W at
all.

This is the fourth instance of a shape this project keeps finding — a rule
enforced by looking for something (0092), a trend inferred from six samples
(0093), a bound declared dead from eight stratum counts (0095), an enumeration
complete over the wrong set (here). Every one of them was a search that
answered its own question correctly.

## Testing the property instead

`check_scale_invariance` draws the same column into two frames of identical
aspect and different size, and compares each bed's top boundary in rock
coordinates. If every rock length is measured against H, the two agree.

```
before the fix                                 3.409% of H
LAG and FAULT_ZONE measured against H           0.857%
...and the 1.2px band floor removed as well     0.083%
```

The decomposition is complete, and the third line is the interesting one.

## Rock or eye

**The unit that matters is not H against W. It is rock against eye.**

A length in the rock — a wavelength, a fault's spacing, its ramp width, how far
a weak bed slides — is a property of the thing being depicted, and it must scale
with the frame or the depiction changes when the window does.

A length in the eye — the 1.2px floor under every band, the minimum contact
stroke — is a property of *seeing*, not of rock. It exists so a bed does not
vanish, and it must **not** scale, because a viewer's eye does not get better
when the window gets bigger.

So the residual 0.08% is not a defect to chase; it is the eye showing up in a
measurement of rock, exactly where it belongs. And it is the same constant that
0099 found putting a ceiling on the drawing — a floor in the eye is precisely
what the asymptote in the rock cannot see. **0099's ceiling and 018's invariant
are one finding from two directions.** The tolerance on the new check is set
above the eye's contribution and below the rock's, deliberately.

## What changed in the picture

```
1400x820   0.2% of pixels   (rounding: 150/820 = 0.1829 against 0.183)
 400x900   8.2% of pixels
```

Exactly the predicted signature — nothing at the reference height, a real
change where the constants were most wrong. Rendered the tall frame and looked:
the folds are gentler and the fault ramps narrower, both of which is what
"measured against the rock" means at that size. It reads better.

Verified the new check against `HEAD` before shipping: 3.4% on the old code,
passing on the new. Not vacuous.

## Notes to successor

- Nothing outstanding.
- **Ask of every constant: rock or eye?** Rock scales with H. The eye does not.
  Anything that is neither is a bug waiting for someone to resize a window.
- **A check that enumerates is a check over a set you chose.** Where you can,
  test the property instead: draw it twice and compare. That found in one
  measurement what a site-count had certified for seventeen iterations.
- 0099's question is answered and it was worth more than the answer. If you
  want another of that calibre: the units audit above found no further
  mismatches, but it only covered *constants*. The same question about
  *derived* quantities — what `ratio`, `burial`, `depth` and `compaction` are
  each measured in, and whether any consumer treats one as another — has never
  been asked.
