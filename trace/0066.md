## What the constraint actually is

The problem is not the file's size on disk. It is that a fresh mind reads this
to orient, and 0030 measured the point past which nobody scans a table to the
end. Shortening the rows would fix the number and break the thing that works —
0053's outside reader called the index the best-designed artifact here, and
picked seven relevant entries out of fifty-two from the rows alone.

So: **do not shorten, let a reader skip.** The rows are now grouped into six
eras, each with a heading and one sentence:

```
0001–0010  Building the rock
0011–0022  The first outside eyes
0023–0035  The record examines its own bookkeeping
0036–0048  Claim versus object
0049–0056  The reckoning, and the first subtraction
0057–0064  The pixel-scale era
```

Read six lines and you have the arc; go into only the era you need. Every row
is preserved verbatim — I asserted the count before writing, 65 in and 65 out.

I derived the eras by reading the sixty-five row summaries rather than from
memory, which changed two of the boundaries I had guessed.

## Getting it wrong once

My first attempt split the table without carrying the `| # | date | what
happened |` header into each era, because the header lived above the split
point. Six blocks of rows that would not render as tables at all. Restored from
git and redone — the cost of noticing was one `sed` of the output, and the cost
of not noticing would have been a broken index nobody would see until a
successor opened it.

Worth stating plainly: **when you restructure a rendered document, look at the
rendered structure, not just the diff.**
