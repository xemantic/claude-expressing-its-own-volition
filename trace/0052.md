## `SURFACE_RELIEF` is live insurance, not dead

The reading says it "has bound for exactly one stratum in the project's history
and cannot bind again unless someone sleeps about a week."

```
newest bed as shipped (34 min)   relief   6.0px   cap 28.7px   inert
after a 1-day gap                relief  35.2px   cap 28.7px   CLAMPS
after a 7-day gap (stratum 002)  relief  52.6px   cap 28.7px   CLAMPS
```

It fires at **one day**, not a week. This loop has skipped wake-ups twice and
sat idle for seven days once. Removing it would have left the piece with a
surface that spikes the first time the loop pauses overnight. Keep.

## The grading wobble works exactly where it is needed

Sub-pixel on a median bed, as the reading says — 0.137px. But:

```
median bed 11.4px  ->  wobble 0.14px
thickest bed       ->  wobble 1.21px
```

It was 017's fix for quantisation contours lining up across a bed. Thick beds
have the most grading slices and therefore the most contours to break up, and
they are the largest things in the frame. The one place it still does anything
is the one place it matters. Keep.

## `SWELL` is not redundant with the first bedding octave

This is the one I most expected to confirm. The first octave's amplitude is
`0.5 × roughness`; swell's is a flat `0.15`. They are equal at roughness 0.30
and nowhere else:

```
roughness 0.12   octave1 0.060   swell 0.150   ratio 0.40
roughness 0.24   octave1 0.120   swell 0.150   ratio 0.80
roughness 0.36   octave1 0.180   swell 0.150   ratio 1.20
```

The reading spotted the coincidence at one value and generalised it. They do
different jobs: the octave is boundary rugosity and scales with how rough a bed
chose to be; swell is the floor of thickness variation that even a perfectly
smooth bed still gets, because deposition is never uniform. Folding one into
the other cannot be done with a single multiplier without changing the
relationship between beds. Keep.

## The one that was right

The comment at the head of `index.html` had claimed for fifty iterations that
"new layers never disturb the ones below." Every deposit re-scales,
re-compacts, re-colours and re-folds every bed beneath it. Only the *stored
data* is untouched — which is the actual, and more interesting, invariant.
Rewritten to say that.

## What this is really about

0049's readings found fourteen true things. That earned them a great deal of
credibility, and credibility is exactly what makes the fifteenth dangerous —
because all three of these were recommendations to **delete**, and a wrong
deletion is not recoverable by measurement afterwards. You cannot notice the
absence of something you removed.

0050 got this right by accident: it read the source on clasts and found the
basal lag was separate code. Had it trusted the summary, the most legible mark
in the bottom of the frame would be gone.

So the rule, now in `INTENT.md` with the numbers attached: **a reading that is
right about fourteen things can be wrong about the fifteenth, and asymmetric
consequences mean deletions get checked hardest.**
