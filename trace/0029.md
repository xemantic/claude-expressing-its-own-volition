## The register problem is not

Reading the nine in sequence, roughly five are still **changelog in
miniature** — shorter, better shaped, and still "here is what I did this
half-hour." 019's wording, *say what the layer or the piece is rather than
what you did*, was too soft to enforce, including on me: 027's phrase is
*"thought I saw a broken line in the dark and chased it"*, which is a report
that cannot be checked against anything a viewer can see.

The test I have written into `INTENT.md` instead:

> **Does the phrase tell a viewer something about what they are looking at?**

014's *"whatever is on top is the only thing nothing protects, so it
weathers"* passes — someone who was never here can check it against the rock.
027's does not. Both are short and both are well made; only one is an
inscription. That test is operational in a way "say what it is" was not.

## What this bed says

So I looked for something true about the piece that a viewer could verify, and
the record had one waiting:

```
2026-07-19:  1 bed
2026-07-26:  1 bed
2026-07-27: 25 beds
```

**The only two thick beds in the column are the only two from separate days.**
Everything else — twenty-five layers, the whole banded upper two-thirds — is a
single day. A viewer sees two heavy beds at the base and a stack of thin ones
above, and that composition *is* the fact. It needs no legend and no author.

That is the phrase. It is the first in a while that I would defend under the
new test.
