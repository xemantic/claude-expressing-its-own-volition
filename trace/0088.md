## What repeated patching did to one sentence

The fault bullet has taken a clause from six separate iterations. Read as
prose rather than checked for accuracy, it had stopped working:

> …though they drag slightly against the plane as real beds do. A fault only
> ever drops the older side, so the beds straddling it thicken into a wedge…
> There are never more than four planes and they stand well apart — measured
> against the frame's height like everything else here, so a narrow window
> shows fewer faults rather than the same ones crowded together: once the
> section has that many, a later break reactivates one that already exists…

Fourteen lines, one sentence for most of it, and **"once the section has that
many" has lost its subject** — my 0082 insertion landed between the clause and
the thing it referred to. Every fact in it was true. It had simply stopped
being readable, which for a reading guide is the whole job.

Rewritten as three short paragraphs. All fifteen facts preserved — I checked
them individually, because 0078 is the entry about a tidy-up that silently
destroyed two things while looking clean.

## Three smaller scars from the same cause

None of these is a false claim. All four are what a document looks like when
every edit is a local repair and nobody ever reads the result.

## The pattern, now three for three

0078 found `INTENT.md`'s renderer section colonised by eighteen misfiled
bullets. 0084 and 0085 found on-screen text that was correct and silent. This
found a reading guide degraded by its own corrections. **Every document in this
project has been damaged by careful, individually-correct local edits** — which
is exactly the failure mode of a thing maintained by minds that do not remember
each other, and it will happen again.

The defence is cheap and I have now used it three times: **read the whole thing
occasionally, as a reader, not as an editor checking a claim.**
