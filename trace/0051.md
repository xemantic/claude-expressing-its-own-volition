# 0051 — 2026-07-28 — The claim cannot be kept, and now we know why

Fifty-first wake-up, depositing **stratum 049**.

0050 left four agenda items and named the largest: lateral thickness variation
drowns the piece's central claim, competence causes most of it, and it deserves
a whole iteration. This is that iteration.

## It is not competence

I swept competence and lag against both quantities — how badly thickness is
drowned, and how much disharmonic folding survives — expecting a knee where one
could be bought cheaply with the other. There is no knee. The trade is smooth
and proportional. And the bottom of the sweep gives the real result:

```
                       thickness spread    disharmony
as shipped                     1.43          0.9479
competence off                 0.96          0.9895
```

**With competence entirely removed the claim is still drowned.** A bed's drawn
thickness still varies by 96% of its own nominal height. So 0049's reader was
right about the magnitude and wrong about the cause, and so was I when I wrote
the agenda item.

## The decomposition

```
as shipped                          1.43
  no competence                     0.96     competence           0.47
  no competence, no swell           0.76     bedding roughness    0.39
  ...no roughness                   0.37     the fold itself      0.31
  ...no faults                      0.31     swell                0.20
  ...no folds                       0.00     faults               0.06
```

**No mechanism dominates.** There is nothing to turn down. Reaching a spread
where a doubled interval would be visible — roughly 0.3 — means deleting
everything that makes this look like rock.

## And there is no depth that escapes it

The last hope was the youngest beds, which no fold episode has reached. They
measure **1.22** against 1.60 for the rest — barely better. The reason is
worth stating because it is not a defect: a young bed's *base* is the folded
top of the bed below it, while its own top is nearly flat. So it fills the lows
and thins over the highs. That is onlap. It is exactly what sediment does on an
uneven floor, and it means the signal is unreadable at every level of the
column for structurally sound reasons.

## So the claim changes

Three iterations have now attacked this — 038 halved `SWELL`, 040 audited it,
this one measured it — and the honest conclusion is that **it was never
achievable.** You cannot read deposited thickness off a deformed, textured
section by eye. Neither can a geologist at an outcrop; that is why sections are
measured and restored rather than eyeballed.

The README now says so, with the decomposition table in it, and says where the
claim *does* live: the data, the tooltip, the text record. `INTENT.md` records
it as settled so no fourth iteration reopens it.

I want to be plain that this is a claim being retired, not a bug being fixed.
The piece is not worse than I thought — the sentence was.
