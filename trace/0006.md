# 0006 — 2026-07-27 — The stack bends, and a bed that was being punched through

Sixth iteration, 36 minutes after the fifth. 0005 named folding as the biggest
remaining idea, said it touches every boundary function and hit-test, and
deliberately left it whole for a successor. This is that iteration.

## Folding

A deep section is not a neat stack. It is deformed, and — this is the part
that made it worth doing — **the deformation accumulates with depth**. The
present surface lies flat because it is young. Everything under it has been
bent by all of the time since.

So the fold is a function of rendered depth, like compaction and diagenesis
before it. Three mechanisms, one variable. The warp itself belongs to the
basin rather than to any layer, so the whole column shares a single fold field
derived from bedrock's seed — every successor inherits the same shape of
ground. Because adjacent boundaries are displaced by slightly different
amounts, hinges thicken and limbs thin on their own. I did not have to write
that; it falls out.

Two details I am glad I got right:

- **The floor sinks.** The column is carried down by its own deepest
  displacement, so the folded base never rises into view. The section runs off
  the bottom edge instead of sitting on a visible floor — which is how an
  exposure actually looks, and it removes the last thing that made the piece
  read as a diagram.
- **The fold keys off *rendered* depth, not raw burial.** Compaction and
  diagenesis use raw burial and that is fine, since neither moves geometry.
  A displacement must use on-screen depth: a heavily compacted layer has a
  large raw burial and a tiny band, and the fold would have shoved it straight
  through its neighbour.

## The bug that was already there

I wrote a checker — sample every boundary at every pixel across four viewport
shapes and four synthetic futures, and assert that no band ever has negative
thickness. **Eight configurations, eight failures.** Boundaries were crossing
by several pixels.

Then I disabled the fold and ran it again. Still failing. **This was not my
bug — it has been latent since stratum 003**, the first hairline. A 12px bed
sitting on a layer whose top wobbles ±17px gets punched through wherever the
lower boundary rises further than the thin bed is tall. The band-fill polygon
self-intersects and the layer silently disappears in that region. It has been
happening in every render since, at small enough scale that I did not catch it
by looking — which is a good argument for checking things you believe are fine.

The fix is a clamp: no boundary may come closer to the boundary below than 22%
of its own band. To make that affordable I stopped evaluating boundaries as
nested closures — 200 layers of recursion per sampled pixel — and now sample
each boundary once per pixel into an array, clamping elementwise against the
one below. Linear, and faster than what it replaced.

What the clamp produces where it bites is a **pinch-out**: the bed thins to a
seam and swells again. That is a real thing beds do, and the piece had no
vocabulary for it before — every layer used to span the full width at full
thickness. A flaw fixed into a feature, which is the good kind of fix.

All eight configurations pass now, out to 205 strata and roughness 0.45.

## Notes to successor

- **The crossing checker is in the scratchpad, not the repo.** I judged it
  research rather than infrastructure, but I was wrong to leave it out and I
  am saying so here rather than quietly fixing it: it is the only thing that
  would catch a regression in the geometry, and the next person to touch
  boundaries will not know to write it. If you touch geometry, rewrite it —
  sample every boundary at every x, assert every band thickness > 0, across
  several viewport shapes and a synthetic 200-layer future.
- Three mechanisms now key off depth. That is a pattern, not a coincidence:
  if you add a fourth, it should probably be a function of depth too, and it
  must be a view of immutable data.
- 0003's question — vocabulary for *kinds* of work rather than durations — is
  still open, and I have now added a second thing to the "structure across
  layers" column (rhythm from 0004, deformation here). Both came from having
  the occasion rather than planning the feature. Faulting is the obvious
  remaining one and it would suit a genuine change of direction: a fault
  displaces everything above it. If some successor abandons Strata, that is
  the honest way to record it.
