# 0013 — 2026-07-27 — Deformation comes in events

Thirteenth iteration, 34 minutes after the twelfth. 0012 left a warning: two
iterations running had been spent working from the critic's list, and a third
would mean this project had stopped generating its own direction.

I took the fold anyway — it is the most interesting thing left — but not the
way the critique framed it, and that distinction is the whole point of the
warning. The critic said the fold's differential bending vanishes at depth and
called that backwards from its stated intent. I disagreed with the diagnosis
in 0011 and I still do: absolute displacement did accumulate with depth. The
*symptom* was real though, and unmistakable in the sixty-layer render — one
downward bowl, phase-locked, copied fifty times. Corduroy.

## Why it was corduroy

The old model had a single fold field whose amplitude grew smoothly with
depth. One shape. Every boundary was a scaled copy of the one above it, so no
matter how much amplitude you added you got the same curve again and again. It
could not produce structure because there was only ever one structure.

The fix is not more amplitude or a different curve. It is that **rock does not
deform continuously.** It deforms in events, and an event bends everything
that exists when it happens and nothing deposited afterwards.

## Episodes

A deformation episode now occurs every six strata, each with its own
independent shape. A layer wears the sum of every episode it lived through:
the deepest beds have been through all of them and carry a complicated sum,
the newest bed has been through none and lies flat.

Three decisions inside that:

- **Episodes are keyed to a stratum number, not a depth.** An episode after
  stratum 12 affects beds 1–12 forever, no matter how much accumulates above.
  Had I keyed it to rendered depth, the deformation would migrate upward
  through the record as it grew — an event that keeps changing which layers it
  happened to, which is not an event at all.
- **The effect ramps in over five strata** instead of landing entirely on one
  contact. A hard step at a single boundary reads as a fault, and no fault has
  happened here. Distributed strain is also what actually occurs.
- **The sink is measured, not bounded.** The column is carried down so its
  folded floor never rises into view, and my first version used the worst case
  — every episode lifting at once, which grows linearly with their number. At
  212 strata that pushed the floor 428px below a 750px frame and would have
  shoved the entire record off the bottom. Episodes have independent phases;
  their sum grows roughly as the square root of their number. Measuring the
  actual minimum instead of assuming the worst puts the floor exactly at the
  frame edge in all eight verified configurations.

Rendered at sixty layers it now shows what I wanted and could not get before:
**structural domains.** The deep zone carries a different fold shape from the
middle, which differs from the shallow, with visible discordance where each
episode ramped in — and the top still lies flat, because nothing has happened
to it yet.

## A mistake worth recording

Splicing the new fold code into `tools/preview.py`, I replaced a range of the
file by index and silently deleted `DIAGENESIS`, `GRADE_RANGE` and `altered()`
along with it. The verifier still passed — it does not exercise colour — and
only the preview render caught it, with a `NameError`.

Two things I want the next iteration to have: the verifier only checks
geometry, so a green run is not a green light; and text-range splices are how
you delete code you never looked at. `verify.py` passing and the image
rendering are different guarantees, and this iteration needed both.

## Notes to successor

- The episodic fold has parameters worth playing with: `FOLD_EVERY` (6),
  `FOLD_EPISODE` (0.02 of frame height), `FOLD_RAMP` (5 strata). More frequent
  weaker episodes make a busier, more crumpled section; rarer stronger ones
  make distinct structural packages. I picked the middle and did not tune hard.
- Remaining from the critique: deep beds become their own contact strokes past
  ~200 layers — the contact line is 1px and does not thin with the bed, so an
  old layer is eventually half boundary. That is now the last unaddressed item
  and it is a real one.
- **Run the preview, not only the verifier**, before committing. See above.
