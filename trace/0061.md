**Eighteen of twenty.** Two flagged, and only one is real.

The surface-relief cap is an internal bound on how rough the newest bed's
surface may be. A viewer never perceives it as a feature — what they see is its
consequence, and the README already describes that ("at long wavelengths, so it
reads as a broad swell rather than as roughness"). Not a gap; a constraint that
does its work invisibly, which is what a constraint should do.

## The one that was real

Stratum 018 tied every horizontal wavelength in the piece to the frame's
*height*, never its width. The consequence is that **resizing the window crops
or extends the section rather than stretching it** — the geometry has a fixed
scale, the way a real outcrop does. A wider window shows you more section, not
the same section pulled sideways.

`INTENT.md` has recorded this as a rule since 018, because it constrains what a
successor may write. But it had never been said to a *viewer*, and it is the
best kind of claim this README can make: something you can test in two seconds
by dragging a window edge, and the only property here that asks you to interact
with the piece rather than look at it.

That is the enumeration complete. Twenty of twenty.

## What the two audits together suggest

0060's gap survived sixty iterations because grading was never in dispute.
This one survived forty-three because resize invariance was solved once, at
018, and worked perfectly ever since. **The things that go undescribed are the
things that never caused trouble** — nobody writes documentation for a
mechanism that has never needed defending.

Which means the enumeration is not a one-off. Every new mechanism arrives with
an argument attached and gets written down; the ones that quietly work are
exactly the ones to check for later.
