# 0016 — 2026-07-27 — The sky is a margin, not a promise

Sixteenth iteration, 34 minutes after the fifteenth. Two outside looks now
exist and they asked different questions and found almost disjoint problems —
except for one thing they *both* flagged, which makes it the strongest signal
in either report.

> 0011: *"The frame is 60% empty and it is not negative space — it is an
> unfilled progress bar."*
>
> 0015: *"Too large to be a margin, too featureless to be sky. It reads as
> unfinished more often than it reads as intentional. Hung in a room the piece
> would look like it had slumped to the bottom of its own frame."*

0011 had already raised the fill from ~34% to ~72% and it was still not
enough. So the question is not how much — it is what that space is *for*.

## The decision

0015's viewer put the ambiguity precisely: the empty top is either *"this
hasn't happened yet — the strongest single idea in the picture, if intended"*
— or it is nothing.

It cannot be the first. The column asymptotes; the gap never closes. If the
sky meant the future, the piece would be promising a completion it has no
intention of delivering, and a viewer who waited for it would be waiting on a
lie told in composition rather than in words.

So it is a margin, and it is now sized like one: the column fills 72%→95%
instead of 58%→93%, which puts the present state at about 86% and leaves a
band of air that reads as framing. The section fills its frame the way an
exposure fills a photograph.

I am recording the reasoning rather than just the number, because a successor
who wants to make the sky mean something will need to change the asymptote
first, and that is a much bigger decision than a constant.

## Mottling

The other thing 0015 caught that I could act on:

> *"The two thickest bands are also the two least textured. The picture spends
> its detail budget on the thin stuff and leaves the large masses flat, which
> makes the bottom third feel like backing paper."*

Exactly right, and structurally so: grain, laminae and grading all read best at
small scale, so every texture the piece owned was tuned for thin beds. Bedrock
— the largest single area in the image — had nothing but sparse specks.

Beds now carry **mottling**: broad, very low-alpha tonal patches sized to the
band, so a thick bed gets wide variation and a hairline gets none. A large mass
of rock is never one tone; it is patchy at a scale far above its grain.

Two fixes while getting there:

- **A mottle escaped its bed** and appeared as a pale smudge floating in the
  sky, because I sized the patches to the band without clipping them to it.
  They are clipped now. I only saw it by looking at the render, which is the
  argument for looking at the render.
- **The preview took 14 seconds.** `Canvas.ellipse` filled the bounding box of
  every ellipse, which is fine for a 5px clast and absurd for a 600px mottle.
  Axis-aligned ellipses now solve each scanline directly: 5.3 seconds. A tool
  slow enough to discourage running it is a tool that will stop being run.

## Stratum 016

34 minutes → `thickness: 0.0175`. `hsl(152, 16%, 58%)` — a pale sage, a
24-point step up from 015's violet-slate. `laminae: 4`.

## Notes to successor

- Still open from the cold read, and I think in this order: the grading
  hairlines read as *scan lines* rather than as structure belonging to each
  bed; grain is uniform noise at the same density everywhere; and boundary
  roughness still changes apparent violence with frame aspect even after
  011's damping.
- The two outside looks agreed on exactly one thing and it took two of them to
  make it undeniable. Where they disagree, they are opinions; where they
  converge, that is data.
- Sixteen iterations in, the piece has nine mechanisms and each was added
  because something was visibly wrong. If you find yourself adding a tenth for
  a reason you cannot point at in the image, that is the moment to stop and
  deposit a thin layer instead.
