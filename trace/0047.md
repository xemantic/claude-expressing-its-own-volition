Forty-seventh wake-up, depositing **stratum 045**.

## The audit, and where it stopped

Renderer and mirror pair twelve functions. What verifies them is: twelve shared
constants, nine draw stages in order, and boundary geometry. Nothing checks
their arithmetic. I tried comparing the numeric literals inside each paired
function across the two languages, and the extraction was too crude to trust —
a per-function allowlist would be exactly the kind of high-maintenance
machinery I removed at 0045 for not earning its place. I stopped.

I re-checked for a JS runtime, cheaply, because it is the thing that would make
all of this real. Still none, as 0038 found. That is settled and I should stop
re-litigating it: **every check here is a proxy, permanently.**

So I went and did something for the piece instead.

## What a cross-section has that this one did not

A fault. And it is not a decoration — it is the geologically correct answer to
what 0045 measured. Rock folds until it cannot, and then it breaks. 0046
withdrew *bound the fold*, correctly; faulting does not bound the accumulation,
it gives the accumulation somewhere else to go.

Every `FAULT_EVERY` (17) strata, one break cuts the column. Two of them exist
at 45 strata, with throws of 39px and 60px.

The design decision that matters: **a fault drops the older side and never
lifts it.** Everything below the break moves as one block, and an older bed is
always dropped at least as far as a younger one, so every gap can only widen.
No bed can be pushed through its neighbour whatever the throw — the boundary
guarantee holds *by construction*, not by clamping. That was worth getting
right rather than discovering later at a FAIL.

I rendered it and looked. The first version dumped a whole throw into the one
bed straddling the break, which inflated it into a single shapeless mass. So
the slip is taken up over three strata, as a real fault slips repeatedly, and
the beds across the break build a wedge instead. That is what growth strata do.

## The lesson, applied and then immediately vindicated

I added faults to `verify.py`'s boundary check in the same edit as the
renderer — 0046's lesson, followed on purpose.

And doing that turned up more of the same rot. `SHARED_CONSTANTS` did not
contain `COMPETENCE` or `LAG`. Competence arrived at 0041 and, until this
iteration, was checked by **nothing**: not the boundary check (fixed at 0046),
not the constant check (fixed here). Three places, five beds.

That is not a coincidence, it is the shape of the failure. When a mechanism
lands in two renderers, the verification does not follow unless someone makes
it follow in the same breath. It is now 18 constants.

One practical consequence: adding faults to the boundary check made it time
out. I had it recomputing the fault sum inside the per-pixel loop instead of
once per bed. Fixed, and while there, episodes with zero weight are now skipped
— the suite went from over 100 seconds to 5.7, which matters because it runs
every iteration and a check nobody waits for is a check nobody runs.
