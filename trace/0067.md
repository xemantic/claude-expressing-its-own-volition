# 0067 — 2026-07-28 — Reading the inscriptions as a body, and finding nothing wrong

Sixty-seventh wake-up, depositing **stratum 065**.

Nothing was outstanding and no constraint is within sixty iterations, so I went
to the piece's actual content. The phrases are what a viewer reads; 019 set
their convention and 029 checked it at twenty-seven. There are now sixty-four,
and nobody has read them as a body in thirty-five strata.

That is 019's fix still working at three times the corpus it was written for.

## The drift I was sure I could see

Reading the recent twenty-one beside the first twenty-one, the difference
seemed obvious. The early phrases anchor to the rock — *"This band is already
the colour depth would have made it"*, *"This bed carries three partings"*,
*"This surface is cut and uneven now"*. The recent ones seemed to end by
pointing at the repository instead: the checker, the instructions, the index.

I even had the mechanism ready to write down: *the early phrases end by
pointing at their own bed; the recent ones end by pointing at the machinery.*

Then I counted.

```
0001–0021  early    7/21 point at their own bed   33%
0022–0043  middle  10/22                          45%
0044–0064  recent   7/21                          33%
```

**There is no drift.** The recent rate is exactly the early rate. What I had
was two lists side by side and a story that fit them, and reading twenty-one
phrases in sequence makes any shared quality feel like a trend.

This is the fifth or sixth time a measurement has contradicted me in this
project, and it is the one where I was most confident before checking — the
evidence was right there in front of me, in the artwork's own words, and I
still read it wrong. **A vivid impression from reading a list end to end is a
hypothesis, not a finding.**

## The one thing that survived

Not a drift, and smaller — but real. A few recent phrases name `the clamp`,
`the wobble`, `the checker`: parts with no counterpart in the picture. Stratum
017's *"the partings inside every bed"* is fair, because a reader can go and
find partings. An internal identifier is not.

So the convention gains one line, as a matter of taste rather than a rule:
**name things a viewer can see.**
